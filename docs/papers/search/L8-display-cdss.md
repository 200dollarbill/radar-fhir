**Abstract:** Electronic medical records (EMRs) often suffer from poor clinician usability resulting in frustration, inefficiency, and adverse events. EMRs also usually display relevant clinical information, such as vital signs or medication administration, in different areas of the chart, rather than grouping relevant clinical information together in intervention-centric or diagnosis-centric views. Safe and effective inpatient diuresis of acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF) patients requires synthesizing clinical information frequently found in different areas of the EMR. We created a clinician-friendly diuresis dashboard in the EMR to assist providers in treating patients with ADHF and determined its impact on usability and efficiency.

Methods: We conducted an observational study pre- and post-implementation of the diuresis dashboard. Pre-implementation, we surveyed providers who treat ADHF patients and routinely place orders in the EMR on their perceptions of EMR use for ADHF patients. We then observed them review information in the EMR prior to placing a one-time diuretic order. We measured the amount of time elapsed and the number of clicks. Following implementation of the diuresis dashboard, we repeated the survey and observational data collection.

Results: Pre-implementation, 30 providers were observed treating 34 patients, and post-implementation, 27 providers were observed treating 30 patients. On a 5-point Likert scale, providers reported a significant increase in both efficiency of information retrieval (3.88 vs 2.73, p=0.0034) and EMR satisfaction (4.24 vs 2.87, p=1.4×10-6) post-implementation of the diuresis dashboard. Providers were observed to make 43% fewer clicks (8.9 vs 15.6, p=1.5×10-7) and spent 23% less time reviewing information per patient (75 vs 98 seconds, p=0.0026).

Conclusion: Creation of a clinician-oriented intervention-centric diuresis dashboard for treating ADHF patients increased provider satisfaction and efficiency with the EMR. Intervention-centric and diagnosis-centric dashboards should be used to improve EMR usability and have the potential to improve patient care.

**Abstract:** OBJECTIVE: Clinician information overload is prevalent in critical care settings. Improved visualization of patient information may help clinicians cope with information overload, increase efficiency, and improve quality. **Abstract:** IMPORTANCE: Duplicated text is a well-documented hazard in electronic medical records (EMRs), leading to wasted clinician time, medical error, and burnout. This study hypothesizes that text duplication is prevalent and increases with time and EMR size and that duplicate information is shared across authors.

OBJECTIVE: To examine the prevalence and scope of duplication behavior in clinical notes from a large academic health system and the factors associated with duplication.

DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS: This retrospective, cross-sectional analysis of note length and content duplication rates used a set of 10 adjacent word tokens (ie, a 10-gram) sliding-window approach to identify spans of text duplicated exactly from earlier notes in a patient's record for all inpatient and outpatient notes written within the University of Pennsylvania Health System from January 1, 2015, through December 31, 2020. Text duplicated from a different author vs text duplicated from the same author was quantified. Furthermore, novel text and duplicated text per author for various note types and author types, as well as per patient record by number of notes in the record, were quantified. Information scatter, another documentation hazard, was defined as the inverse of novel text per note, and the association between information duplication and information scatter was graphed. Data analysis was performed from January to March 2022.

MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES: Total, novel, and duplicate text by note type and note author were determined, as were the mean intra-author and inter-author duplication per note by type and author.

RESULTS: There were a total of 104 456 653 notes for 1 960 689 unique patients consisting of 32 991 489 889 words; 50.1% of the total text in the record (16 523 851 210 words) was duplicated from prior text written about the same patient. The duplication fraction increased year-over-year, from 33.0% for notes written in 2015 to 54.2% for notes written in 2020. Of the text duplicated, 54.1% came from text written by the same author, whereas 45.9% was duplicated from a different author. Records with more notes had more total duplicate text, approaching 60%. Note types with high information scatter tended to have low information overload, and vice versa, suggesting a trade-off between these 2 hazards under the current documentation paradigm.

CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: Duplicate text casts doubt on the veracity of all information in the medical record, making it difficult to find and verify information in day-to-day clinical work. The findings of this cross-sectional study suggest that text duplication is a systemic hazard, requiring systemic interventions to fix, and simple solutions such as banning copy-paste may have unintended consequences, such as worsening information scatter. The note paradigm should be further examined as a major cause of duplication and scatter, and alternative paradigms should be evaluated.

**Abstract:** Aim: Despite strong evidence for guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT), variation in heart failure (HF) care persists across Australian hospitals. Electronic medical record (EMR)-based dashboards may support optimisation of therapy and service delivery, yet many are developed without sustained clinician and consumer input. We aimed to co-design a HF dashboard with complementary service-level and clinical views for implementation within Australian hospitals.

Method: A human-centred design framework (empathise, define, ideate, prototype, test) was applied across two NSW Local Health Districts, in collaboration with the eHealth NSW Human Centred Design team. Semi-structured interviews, collaborative synthesis, ideation workshops and iterative prototype testing were conducted with cardiologists, junior doctors, HF nurses, executives, analysts and consumers with lived experience of HF. Successive digital prototypes were refined based on structured feedback and thematic analysis.

Results: Thirty stakeholders participated across two co-design streams. Service-level participants prioritised real-time visibility of patient location, service engagement, readmissions, mortality and site-level GDMT adherence to inform resourcing and quality improvement. Clinicians prioritised current and historical GDMT status, recent investigations, functional status and identification of unmet therapy opportunities to support ward-based decision-making and discharge planning. Consumers emphasised fragmented communication between hospital and primary care, repeated recounting of medical histories, and inconsistent education regarding medications and self-management. Across streams, workflow integration and data accuracy, security, and providence were considered critical for adoption.

Conclusion: Collaborative design activities produced a dual-view HF dashboard linking service-level performance with patient-level decision support. This work provides a clinician- and consumer-informed blueprint for implementation and prospective trial in Australian hospitals.
